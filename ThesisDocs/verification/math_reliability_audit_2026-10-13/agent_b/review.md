### D1 — Model, filtration, admissibility, selector and costs

B-CH2-01–04/06, chapter2_theory.md:5–65,78–82. For a measurable causal answer A_t, binary grading C_t is a bounded random variable, even when hidden from the controller. The cumulative-history sigma-fields are nested. All controller randomness and executed information must be included, while a feature-only information model must restrict policies to that information. If the task reveals a deterministic measurable reference, the reference and correctness are measurable regardless of computational difficulty; then q_t=C_t. The manuscript correctly states this often missed degeneracy. Its nondegenerate alternatives are assumptions, not evidence that a fitted head is the full-history posterior.

A finite nonempty observable candidate set with measurable enumeration admits a measurable maximum conditional correctness reward and a measurable tie-breaking selector. The displayed maximum describes optimal immediate selection, not free prediction of future candidates. A selected-answer process must be fixed before defining transitions. A full potential trace is valid for stopping-only comparison when its law agrees with executed prefixes. Changing prompts or allocation changes that law. Finite horizon and integrability of each K_t imply integrability at every bounded stop: |G_tau| <= 1+sum_t K_t. Nondecreasing cost is needed for stake monotonicity and cost interpretations, not for the basic finite Snell recursion. An empty decision range m=N is legitimate and forces tau=N.

### D2 — Random-time observable reward reduction

B-CH2-05, chapter2_theory.md:67–76. The finite partition {tau=t} consists of F_t events. Thus E[1_{tau=t}C_t]=E[1_{tau=t}E(C_t|F_t)]=E[1_{tau=t}q_t]. Sum over m<=t<=N and subtract E K_tau. This avoids the invalid shortcut E(C_tau|F_tau)=q_tau without first defining the stopped sigma-field. No independence or Markov assumption is used. The same argument applies to the frozen binary grading predicate; it establishes an identity about that predicate, not semantic label correctness.

### D3 — Repair-corruption drift and martingale decomposition

B-CH2-07–09, chapter2_theory.md:86–132. Conditional on F_t, r_t<=1-q_t and s_t<=q_t, so the nonnull ratios lie in [0,1]. On q_t=1, r_t=0; on q_t=0, s_t=0. Any [0,1] null-state hazard has zero multiplier. The pointwise difference C_{t+1}-C_t is the repair indicator minus the corruption indicator. Nested conditioning gives E(q_{t+1}|F_t)=E(C_{t+1}|F_t), hence mu_t=r_t-s_t-c_t=(1-q_t)alpha_t-q_t beta_t-c_t. The residual increment G_{t+1}-G_t-mu_t has conditional mean zero. Finite summation proves the claimed integrable martingale M_t with M_m=0. Since C_t changes with the selected candidate, q_t need not be a martingale. For example surely wrong then surely right yields q=(0,1).

### D5 — Affine stakes

B-CH2-11, chapter2_theory.md:152–175. vC-p(1-C)=wC-p for w=v+p>0. Conditional reward is wq-p-K, so drift is w(r-s)-c. Dividing the objective by w and dropping -p/w leaves q-K/w. ### D6 — Finite Snell envelope and continuation advantage

B-CH2-12/13, chapter2_theory.md:179–233. Backward recursion produces adapted integrable S, since max of two integrable variables is integrable. It dominates G and satisfies the supermartingale inequalities. If W dominates G and is a supermartingale, induction from N gives W_t>=max(G_t,E(S_{t+1}|F_t))=S_t. For any admissible tau>=t, write S_tau-S_t=sum_{s=t}^{N-1}1_{tau>s}(S_{s+1}-S_s). The indicators are F_s measurable, proving E(S_tau|F_t)<=S_t. Contact is an F-stopping time, is forced by N, and before contact the recursion has equality with conditional continuation. Its stopped process is a martingale, yielding E(G_tau*|F_t)=S_t and attainment of the essential supremum. Equality/ties are resolved by the first contact, not by continuing through ties. This also proves it is the earliest optimal rule: stopping earlier with positive conditional advantage on a positive-probability event could be improved by switching there to the attaining continuation rule.

Subtract G_t from E S_{t+1}: Delta*=mu+E(S_{t+1}-G_{t+1}|F_t)>=mu. At N-1, S_N=G_N, so equality holds. Therefore positive mu implies strict continuation but nonpositive mu alone does not imply contact. At m=N the recursion has only S_N=G_N and no continuation formula is needed.

### D7 — Stake monotonicity, including ties

B-CH2-14, chapter2_theory.md:235–263. The set of admissible rules required to continue once is identical for all fixed w. For each such tau, the normalized advantage is E[q_tau-q_t-(K_tau-K_t)/w|F_t]. Nondecreasing K makes each expression nondecreasing in w; taking essential suprema preserves the inequality. Thus Delta(w1)>0 implies Delta(w2)>0. On a path before the smaller-stakes first contact, every smaller-stakes decision is strictly to continue, hence so is the larger-stakes decision. It cannot stop earlier. At a zero smaller-stakes advantage, it may stop while the larger continues, which is consistent with the ordering. If both advantages are zero, earliest-contact ties stop both. The assumption is fixed positive w with common nondecreasing cost, not arbitrary fitted thresholds or strictly increasing accuracy.

### D8 — Persistence after a random crossing and structural sufficient condition

B-CH2-15/16, chapter2_theory.md:267–326. For a bounded admissible tau, telescope G_tau-G_m using 1_{tau>s}; conditional expectation replaces increments by mu_s. The first crossing T_c is a stopping time because mu_s is F_s measurable and the search is finite. Before T_c its drift is strictly positive, and at/after T_c all drifts are nonpositive by the stated full-path persistence premise. Pointwise, [1_{T_c>s}-1_{tau>s}]mu_s>=0 for each s. Summation establishes E G_Tc>=E G_tau. Randomness of T_c causes no illicit conditioning: the sign comparison is pointwise on a common probability-one event. A population mean crossing is insufficient.

The adjacent-drift expansion has four terms: (1-q_{t+1})(alpha_{t+1}-alpha_t), alpha_t(q_t-q_{t+1}), -q_{t+1}(beta_{t+1}-beta_t), -beta_t(q_{t+1}-q_t). The recorded pathwise monotonicities make all four <=0. Variable predictable cost adds -(c_{t+1}-c_t), so nondecreasing cost is sufficient. This is a strong conditional structural premise; no empirical monotone mean or high AUC proves it.
