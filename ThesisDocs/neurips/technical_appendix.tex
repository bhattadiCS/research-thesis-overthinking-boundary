\section{Full derivations and finite-horizon proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

Throughout this section, time is discrete, \(m,N\) are fixed finite integers,
\((\F_t)_{t=0}^N\) is a filtration, and all random variables are defined on one
probability space. Correctness \(C_t\in\{0,1\}\) need not be observable.
Its conditional expectation \(q_t=\E[C_t\mid\F_t]\) is adapted. Cost \(K_t\)
is adapted and integrable; nondecreasing cost is needed for the stakes
comparison but not for the Snell construction. Versions of conditional
expectations can be chosen consistently outside one null set because only
finitely many times occur. Policies and candidate selection are restricted
to the declared information. All assertions involving inequalities between
conditional quantities hold almost surely.

\subsection{Stopped reward and transition identity}
\begin{lemma}[Observable reward reduction]
\label{lem:reduction-full}
For every stopping time \(m\le\tau\le N\),
\(\E[C_\tau-K_\tau]=\E[q_\tau-K_\tau]\).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each \(t\), the event \(\{\tau=t\}\) belongs to \(\F_t\). Thus
\[
 \E[\ind\{\tau=t\}C_t]
 =\E[\ind\{\tau=t\}\E[C_t\mid\F_t]]
 =\E[\ind\{\tau=t\}q_t].
\]
Sum over \(t=m,\ldots,N\) and subtract the common integrable cost.
No conditional independence or measurability of \(C_t\) itself is required.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Binary conditional drift]
\label{lem:drift-full}
With \(r_t,s_t,\alpha_t,\beta_t,c_t\) defined as in
Section \ref{sec:theory}, \(r_t=(1-q_t)\alpha_t\),
\(s_t=q_t\beta_t\), and Equation \eqref{eq:drift} holds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The conditional repair mass is at most
\(\PP(C_t=0\mid\F_t)=1-q_t\), and the corruption mass is at most \(q_t\).
They therefore vanish on the respective denominator-zero events. The stated
ratio definitions and zero conventions give the mass equalities everywhere
outside a null set, with \(0\le\alpha_t,\beta_t\le1\). Pointwise,
\[
 C_{t+1}-C_t
 =\ind\{C_t=0,C_{t+1}=1\}
  -\ind\{C_t=1,C_{t+1}=0\}.
\]
Since \(\F_t\subseteq\F_{t+1}\), the tower property gives
\(\E[q_{t+1}\mid\F_t]=\E[C_{t+1}\mid\F_t]\).
Subtract \(q_t\) and the conditional cost increment to obtain the identity.
\end{proof}

For finite-sample descriptive transitions, if the same \(n\) trajectories
provide all adjacent binary outcomes, let \(n_{01}\) and \(n_{10}\) be the
repair and corruption counts. Their difference divided by \(n\), rather than
the difference of the two eligible-subgroup rates, is the empirical accuracy
increment. This is an exact counting identity, not a population inference.
An affine reward \(vC_t-p(1-C_t)-K_t\) reduces to
\((v+p)q_t-p-K_t\); its drift is
\((v+p)[(1-q_t)\alpha_t-q_t\beta_t]-c_t\).

\subsection{Proof of Theorem \ref{thm:snell}}
The recursion in Equation \eqref{eq:snell} constructs an adapted integrable
process: finite maxima of integrable variables remain integrable.
It dominates \(G\) and satisfies
\(\E[S_{t+1}\mid\F_t]\le S_t\).
If an integrable supermartingale \(Z\) dominates \(G\), then \(Z_N\ge S_N\).
Backward induction yields
\[
 Z_t\ge G_t,\qquad
 Z_t\ge\E[Z_{t+1}\mid\F_t]\ge\E[S_{t+1}\mid\F_t],
\]
so \(Z_t\ge S_t\). This proves minimality.

Fix a deterministic start \(t\ge m\). First contact
\(\tau_t^*=\inf\{s\in\{t,\ldots,N\}:S_s=G_s\}\) exists since contact holds
at \(N\), and is a stopping time since \(S_s,G_s\) are adapted.
For any bounded \(\tau\ge t\), telescoping the stopped supermartingale gives
\[
 S_\tau=S_t+\sum_{s=t}^{N-1}\ind\{\tau>s\}(S_{s+1}-S_s).
\]
Each indicator is \(\F_s\)-measurable. Conditioning first on \(\F_s\) and
then on \(\F_t\) shows
\(\E[S_\tau\mid\F_t]\le S_t\), and hence
\(\E[G_\tau\mid\F_t]\le S_t\).

Before contact, \(S_s>G_s\), so the recursion forces
\(S_s=\E[S_{s+1}\mid\F_s]\). Consequently, for \(s\ge t\),
\[
 \E[S_{(s+1)\wedge\tau_t^*}\mid\F_s]=S_{s\wedge\tau_t^*}.
\]
This stopped process is a martingale from the chosen start \(t\).
At terminal contact \(S_{\tau_t^*}=G_{\tau_t^*}\); repeated conditional
expectation yields \(S_t=\E[G_{\tau_t^*}\mid\F_t]\).
Every admissible conditional reward is bounded by \(S_t\), and one admissible
reward attains it, proving the essential-supremum representation and optimum.
This also proves the claims for an imposed floor without permitting stops
before that floor. The proof is the finite discrete specialization of
\citet{ferguson,peskir2006}.

Equation \eqref{eq:option} follows by adding and subtracting
\(\E[G_{t+1}\mid\F_t]\). Domination \(S_{t+1}\ge G_{t+1}\) proves the
nonnegative option term; no bound on its size is supplied by an AUC.
If a finite measurable candidate set is available at a stop, replace the
immediate reward by the maximum of its conditional candidate rewards.
This extension requires candidate-specific estimates and does not justify
selecting a candidate using unavailable gold.

\subsection{Compensator and persistent signs}
\begin{lemma}[Finite drift decomposition]
\label{lem:doob-full}
Define
\[
 D_t=\sum_{s=m}^{t-1}\mu_s,\qquad M_t=G_t-G_m-D_t,\qquad t\ge m.
\]
Then \(M\) is an integrable martingale with \(M_m=0\), and for any admissible
\(\tau\),
\begin{equation}
 \E G_\tau=\E G_m+
 \E\sum_{s=m}^{N-1}\ind\{\tau>s\}\mu_s .
 \label{eq:stopped-drift-full}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Adaptation and integrability follow from finite sums.
The next conditional increment of \(M\) is
\(\E[G_{t+1}-G_t-\mu_t\mid\F_t]=0\).
Alternatively, expand \(G_\tau-G_m\) as the stopped telescoping sum;
measurability of \(\ind\{\tau>s\}\) gives Equation
\eqref{eq:stopped-drift-full} directly.
\end{proof}

To prove Proposition \ref{prop:persistence}, first observe that \(T_c\)
is a stopping time because each \(\mu_t\) is \(\F_t\)-measurable.
For every \(s<T_c\), \(\mu_s>0\) and \(\ind\{T_c>s\}=1\).
For every \(s\ge T_c\), the hypothesis gives \(\mu_s\le0\), while
\(\ind\{T_c>s\}=0\). For every competitor \(\tau\), therefore,
\[
 (\ind\{T_c>s\}-\ind\{\tau>s\})\mu_s\ge0
\]
on each path. Sum and apply Equation \eqref{eq:stopped-drift-full} to both
times. Equality can hold for other policies when some drifts vanish;
the proposition claims optimality, not uniqueness.

The structural sufficient condition stated in the main text is proved by
subtracting adjacent expressions:
\begin{align}
 \mu_{t+1}-\mu_t={}&
 (1-q_{t+1})(\alpha_{t+1}-\alpha_t)
 \alpha_t(q_t-q_{t+1}) \nonumber\\
 &-q_{t+1}(\beta_{t+1}-\beta_t)
 -\beta_t(q_{t+1}-q_t)-(c_{t+1}-c_t).
 \label{eq:monotone-drift-full}
\end{align}
Each term is nonpositive under the pathwise conditions
\(q_{t+1}\ge q_t\), \(\alpha_{t+1}\le\alpha_t\),
\(\beta_{t+1}\ge\beta_t\), and \(c_{t+1}\ge c_t\).
Constant cost is a special case. No population-average replacement of these
conditions is used.

\begin{corollary}[Stakes ordering for an unchanged potential process]
\label{cor:stakes-full}
Let \(0<w_1\le w_2\), where \(w=v+p\). Keep the potential trace law,
filtration, candidate selector, floor, horizon and pathwise nondecreasing
cost process fixed. With first-contact tie-breaking, the optimal times
satisfy \(\tau^*(w_1)\le\tau^*(w_2)\) almost surely.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The constant incorrect-answer offset cancels from continuation comparisons.
The normalized advantage of being forced to continue at least once is
\[
 \frac{\Delta_t^*(w)}{w}
 =\esssup_{\tau:\,t+1\le\tau\le N}
 \E\left[q_\tau-q_t-\frac{K_\tau-K_t}{w}\,\middle|\,\F_t\right].
\]
The Snell representation proves this identity by optimizing the remaining
bounded stop after the mandatory increment. Since \(K_\tau-K_t\ge0\),
each expression inside the essential supremum is nondecreasing in \(w\).
So is the supremum. Strict continuation for \(w_1\) implies strict
continuation for \(w_2\) at that same history. If the higher-stakes first
contact preceded lower-stakes contact on any path, this implication at the
earlier time would be contradicted. The common forced terminal stop
handles \(N\).
\end{proof}
Stakes must not change the prompt, generation law, selection, or cost.
The result neither asserts strictly increasing accuracy nor applies
automatically to estimated myopic boundaries.

\section{Common-reference counterexamples}
\label{app:counterexamples}
\paragraph{Delayed repair.}
Let \(Y^*\in\{0,1\}\) have a fair prior. At times zero and one the generator
outputs the surely incorrect symbol 2, and at time two it outputs \(Y^*\).
Before the last response the visible observations reveal no reference value.
The decision filtration is generated by the observed responses; gold is not
an input. Therefore \(q=(0,0,1)\). With \(K_t=t/10\), rewards are
\((0,-1/10,4/5)\). The first immediate drift is negative, but time two is
optimal. A system that instead always outputs symbol 2 has the same time-zero
\((q,\alpha,\beta,c)=(0,0,0,1/10)\), but stopping immediately is optimal.
Thus an exact current posterior/hazard triple need not identify a multistep
optimum. Prefixing either construction by uninformative responses realizes
the example at any desired minimum-step floor.

\paragraph{Unconditional drift misses adaptation.}
Give the joint signal/reference distribution the four atoms
\[
 \PP(A,Y^*=1)=1/5,\quad \PP(A,Y^*=0)=3/10,\quad
 \PP(B,Y^*=1)=3/10,\quad \PP(B,Y^*=0)=1/5 .
\]
At times zero and one the candidate is 1; the signal is revealed at time one.
Its two branches each have probability \(1/2\), so
\(q_0=1/2\), \(q_1=2/5\) on \(A\), and \(q_1=3/5\) on \(B\).
At time two the generator returns \(Y^*\) on \(A\), and the surely incorrect
symbol 2 on \(B\). Hence \(q_2=\ind\{A\}\). With cost \(t/10\),
the expected deterministic-time rewards are \(1/2,2/5,3/10\);
both unconditional reward increments equal \(-1/10\).
The causal policy that continues at zero, then continues on \(A\) and stops
on \(B\), earns
\[
 \tfrac12(1-2/10)+\tfrac12(3/5-1/10)=13/20>1/2 .
\]
Its decision uses only the observed signal, not the future answer or its
correctness. The time-one conditional drift is \(1/2\) on \(A\) and
\(-7/10\) on \(B\); its average is negative. The signal creates the
continuation option missed by the population crossing.

\section{Prediction, calibration and uncertainty conditions}
\label{app:calibration}
\subsection{Compressed information and weighted loss}
If \(Z_t\) is a causal feature of the prefix, conditional transition algebra
also holds given \(\sigma(Z_t)\). It need not give the same conditional
quantity as the full history, and the fields \(\sigma(Z_t)\) at successive
times need not be nested. Bellman compression additionally requires that,
given the compressed state, the conditional future observation, cost and
reward law not depend on omitted history. A correctly specified partially
observed Markov model can instead use its full latent-state posterior.
The portable logistic heads provide neither such model nor sufficiency proof.

For \(0<p<1\), positive weights \(w_1,w_0\), and \(0<r<1\), weighted conditional
Bernoulli loss is
\[
 L(r)=-w_1p\log r-w_0(1-p)\log(1-r).
\]
Its derivative vanishes uniquely at
\[
 r=\frac{w_1p}{w_1p+w_0(1-p)},\qquad
 p=\frac{w_0r}{w_1(1-r)+w_0r},
\]
because its second derivative is strictly positive.
Endpoint optima are interpreted by limits. This inversion concerns an
unconstrained population optimum; regularization, misspecification and
estimated weights still matter. We avoid class weights for the new heads.
AUC is invariant to strictly increasing score transforms, whereas probability
drift thresholds are not. Even a constant \(1/2\) score is marginally
calibrated in a balanced population whose available history fully reveals
correctness. It is not that history's conditional posterior.

\subsection{Algebraic bounds and their premises}
Let all probability arguments lie in \([0,1]\), with genuine pointwise errors
\(|\widehat q-q|\le\epsilon_q\),
\(|\widehat\alpha-\alpha|\le\epsilon_\alpha\),
\(|\widehat\beta-\beta|\le\epsilon_\beta\), and
\(|\widehat c-c|\le\epsilon_c\). Subtraction gives
\[
 \widehat\mu-\mu
 =(1-\widehat q)(\widehat\alpha-\alpha)
 -\widehat q(\widehat\beta-\beta)
 -(\widehat q-q)(\alpha+\beta)-(\widehat c-c),
\]
so the triangle inequality yields
\[
 |\widehat\mu-\mu|
 \le 2\epsilon_q+(1-\widehat q)\epsilon_\alpha
       +\widehat q\epsilon_\beta+\epsilon_c .
\]
For valid rectangular intervals contained in \([0,1]\), exact extrema are
\[
 L=(1-q_U)a_L-q_Ub_U-c_U,\qquad
 U=(1-q_L)a_U-q_Lb_L-c_L .
\]
The drift increases with \(\alpha\) and decreases with \(q,\beta,c\);
the indicated corners attain the extrema. For the direct two-head identity
\(\mu=p_{\mathrm{next}}-q-c\), hypothetical pointwise head errors instead
give \(|\widehat\mu-\mu|\le\epsilon_{\mathrm{next}}+\epsilon_q+\epsilon_c\).
Reported Brier, ECE and AUC do not supply any of these pointwise premises.

\subsection{A scoped sequential sign certificate}
Assume observable upper bounds \(U_t\) satisfy
\(\PP(\exists t\in\{m,\ldots,N-1\}:\mu_t>U_t)\le\delta\).
Let \(\tau_U\) be the first \(U_t\le0\), with terminal fallback.
On the simultaneous coverage event, every nonterminal stop obeys
\(\mu_{\tau_U}\le U_{\tau_U}\le0\). Thus
\[
 \PP(\tau_U<N,\ \mu_{\tau_U}>0)\le\delta .
\]
This is a one-step sign guarantee, not optimality or retained accuracy.
Replacing the target by the full Bellman advantage would require a separately
valid bound for that advantage.

To obtain a conditional Hoeffding construction, name the pre-probe field
\(\mathcal H_t\) and its measurable target \(\theta_t\).
Suppose \(n_t\) is fixed before observing probes, and conditional on
\(\mathcal H_t\), probes \(X_{t,i}\in[a,b]\) are independent, each with mean
\(\theta_t\). Conditional Hoeffding gives
\[
 \PP\left(\theta_t>\overline X_t+
 (b-a)\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\delta_t)}{2n_t}}\ \middle|\ \mathcal H_t\right)
 \le\delta_t .
\]
Taking \(\delta_t=\delta/[r(r+1)]\), \(r=t-m+1\), and summing yields
simultaneous coverage of the \(\theta_t\) targets. Cross-time independence
is unnecessary. Within-time conditional independence and the named means
remain necessary. The post-probe field includes the probes and their costs;
a bound for \(\theta_t\) covers post-probe \(\mu_t\) only when equality or a
justified comparison is established. Fixed-count Hoeffding does not license
adaptively choosing the probe count without further uniformity
\citep{hoeffding1963,howard2021}.

For an elementary sequential check, suppose \(X_i\in[-1,1]\) is adapted to
\(\mathcal G_i\), and the null satisfies
\(\E[X_i\mid\mathcal G_{i-1}]\ge0\) at every \(i\).
For fixed \(0\le\eta<1\),
\[
 E_n^{(\eta)}=\prod_{i=1}^n(1-\eta X_i)
\]
is nonnegative and has next conditional expectation at most its current
value. A fixed convex mixture also starts at one and is a supermartingale.
If \(T\) is its first crossing of \(1/\delta\), bounded optional sampling
gives \(1\ge\E[E_{T\wedge n}]\ge\PP(T\le n)/\delta\); let \(n\) increase.
Hence \(\PP(\sup_n E_n\ge1/\delta)\le\delta\).
This tests the specified conditional-mean null. If instead \(X_i=B\) for all
\(i\), with one fair shared sign, each marginal mean is zero but the ten-factor
product with \(\eta=1/2\) exceeds 20 with probability \(1/2\).
Favorable marginal means alone therefore cannot establish a 5\% guarantee.
Neither sequential construction is claimed for the live fitted controller.

\subsection{Live prompt and controls}
The system message requests a careful mathematical solution; the user prompt
contains the public task, current step of five, and all previously emitted
thought/answer/confidence observations. It asks for one short reasoning or
verification sentence and an updated current answer, using exactly one JSON
object with thought, answer, integer confidence, and boolean stop; the answer
field contains only the current final answer. History is the same observed
prefix for either policy when prefixes agree. Chat-template formatting is
performed by the model tokenizer.

Each generator call ends on a valid complete JSON object, EOS, a 128-token
completion cap, or external cancellation. The full-horizon baseline has the
same response boundary; its policy continues through five responses.
The heuristic requires valid confidence for confidence-based checks and uses
confidence 90, two equal answers, confidence drop 15 and two changes.
The learned controller requires two observations, then stops on
\(\widehat p_{\mathrm{next}}-\widehat q-0.05\le0\) and otherwise continues.
It forces stop at five before calling a nonexistent next head.
The model-reported stop field is recorded telemetry, not an independent
correctness oracle. The default selector carries forward the last nonempty
answer. The heuristic drop branch instead retains the immediately previous
answer, and its wobble branch chooses the highest-confidence valid observed
answer, breaking ties toward the earliest step. Neither branch occurs in
these actual main or trap runs; learned/baseline use the default selector.
No verifier or peer is executed in these experiments.

Controllers accept only consecutive Observation objects, own the prefix,
and reject an append after closure. The generation adapter checks caller
cancellation during decoding.
Optional peer measurements must use same-step complete rosters, reject future
entries and broken barriers, and charge all peer work. Tests check those API
invariants; an API field restriction alone does not prove the statistical
filtration is sufficient. Frozen executed sources and copied predictor bytes
are bound by the run manifests. Greedy batched generation can change when
batch composition changes, so the observed 94/100 heuristic and 100/100
learned shared prefixes are explicitly recorded.

\section{Uncertainty and reproducibility boundaries}
\label{app:uncertainty}
Archived learned replay uses 2,000 paired task-cluster percentile bootstrap
replicates with seed 20261002. All rows of a selected task move together.
Live cost intervals use 10,000 paired task resamples with seed 804, recomputing
\(1-\sum_i T_i^{\mathrm{active}}/\sum_i T_i^{\mathrm{baseline}}\) in each
replicate. They are empirical percentile intervals under resampling assumptions,
not exact distribution-free finite-sample guarantees, and do not include
across-seed generation uncertainty. The selected 52-cell contrasts use cells,
not rows, as their separately declared resampling units.

For paired correctness, define improvement indicator
\(I_i=\ind\{C_i^{\mathrm{active}}=1,C_i^{\mathrm{baseline}}=0\}\)
and worsening indicator
\(W_i=\ind\{C_i^{\mathrm{active}}=0,C_i^{\mathrm{baseline}}=1\}\).
Under an iid task-pair reference model, their totals each have a binomial
marginal distribution, even though \(I_i,W_i\) are mutually exclusive.
Let \([L_I,U_I]\) and \([L_W,U_W]\) be two-sided Clopper--Pearson
intervals at confidence 97.5\%, computed by binomial tail inversion
\citep{clopper1934}. Each marginal interval has coverage at least 97.5\%.
A union bound, without independence of the two margins, gives simultaneous
coverage at least 95\%. On that event
\[
 \PP(I_i=1)-\PP(W_i=1)
 \in[L_I-U_W,\ U_I-L_W].
\]
Main learned counts \(n=100,I=1,W=0\) give
\([-0.0427483,0.0621192]\). Trap counts \(n=20,I=W=0\) give
\([-0.196760,0.196760]\). The latter is an iid-reference calculation for a
