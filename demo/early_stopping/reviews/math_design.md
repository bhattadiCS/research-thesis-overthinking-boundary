## Shared-prefix comparison is mathematically coherent

Generate one response at each of at most five response boundaries, conditioning on the one common observed history. Before the active controller stops, deliver the identical immutable Observation to the active and never-stop controllers. The baseline's generator inputs must not change with active-policy decisions. Once the active decision is STOP, freeze its selected answer, selected candidate step, stop reason, decision time, observed prefix and charged cost. Do not call its `observe` method or predictor again. Continue generating only the baseline's remaining responses until response five.

This constructs a common potential trajectory for a stopping-only comparison. Unlike two separate greedy jobs, common-prefix equality holds by construction. It is valid only while stopping does not otherwise alter prompts, sampling, candidate selection or execution of the observed prefix. It compares two stopping policies on that shared trajectory; it does not produce independent replications or establish expected accuracy or utility in a population.

Let L_s be actual completion tokens generated in response s, T the active stop boundary, A=sum_{s<=T}L_s and B=sum_{s<=5}L_s. The comparison completion-token reduction is (B-A)/B, provided the full baseline completed and B>0. Charge every generated response up to T even if the active selector returns an earlier candidate. The displayed selected candidate step and the executed response count are different quantities.

The actual demo generates B completion tokens once, rather than A+B in two independent jobs. It still executes the full baseline suffix. Label A and B as arm accounting on a shared trajectory and the reduction as completion tokens avoided by the early arm relative to the full horizon. Do not describe B-A as physical computation saved by this demonstration job. Prompt tokens, model time, controller time, energy and completion tokens are different measures. A measured response-boundary stop time can be displayed; an unmeasured two-job speedup or energy saving cannot be inferred from token ratios.

If the baseline is cancelled or fails before five responses, the full-horizon comparison is incomplete. Preserve the observed active STOP if one already occurred, but mark the baseline partial/cancelled and suppress a final full-horizon saving percentage. Model cancellation is a separate outcome from a policy STOP. Do not silently treat an incomplete baseline as the full-horizon denominator.

## Existing selector and controller semantics

The following were read directly from the current research code:

- `research/online_stopping_controller.py:197–254`: observations arrive exactly once, consecutively, with a two-response floor and terminal closure at the configured horizon. The default selector is the latest nonempty `answer` field. The heuristic can instead retain a previous candidate on a confidence drop or the highest-confidence earlier candidate on answer wobble. After STOP, feeding another observation raises an error.
- `research/learned_online_stopping_controller.py:67–103`: learned stopping reuses the never-stop controller's observation and answer-selection invariants. At responses two through four it obtains q_current and p_next and recomputes estimated drift as `(reward_value + wrong_penalty) * (p_next - q_current) - step_cost`. With the frozen constants this is `p_next - q_current - 0.05`. It stops on a nonpositive value. Terminal handling precedes prediction, so response five has no next-step estimate or drift. A changed predictor hash or nonfinite/out-of-range probability is rejected.
- `research/prefix_stopping_model.py:20–42,45–91,117–191`: the frozen artifact supports exactly `gsm8k` and `math`, a minimum of two and maximum of five responses, and causal Observation prefixes. q_current targets correctness of the latest nonempty selected candidate; p_next targets the same selection procedure after one more response. These estimates are not the model's self-reported confidence, a verified correctness label, a Bellman continuation value or a sequential coverage certificate.
- `research/online_generation.py:22–58,108–190`: each generator call produces one complete response bounded by the first recognized valid structured object, EOS or the token cap. The generated `stop` boolean is model telemetry; it does not itself stop the never-stop policy. Strict-parser confidence is available only from a recognized structured object. Fallback parsing can yield a candidate with untrusted/missing confidence. A nonempty raw response is different from a nonempty parsed candidate.

The learned rule can stop on a prefix with an untrusted/fallback parse, because missingness is part of its fitted features. The heuristic requires trusted parsing for its confidence/stability branches. A UI must not silently impose a new parse gate on the learned policy or reuse model-reported `stop` as the policy decision; either change would be a new policy.

The raw `thought` field is a generated short explanation/verification summary at a complete-response boundary. It does not expose all internal reasoning or certify the truth of its intermediate text. Suitable UI terms are “response,” “revision,” “candidate,” and “reasoning summary.”

## Arbitrary prompts and probability interpretation

An arbitrary user prompt has no supplied reference label, so this demo cannot report its correctness or claim that either arm retained accuracy. The only trained domain codes are `gsm8k` and `math`; make the selected domain explicit and keep it fixed for a run. A new prompt outside those settings is an extrapolation of a small-model development predictor. Do not silently map unsupported domain names into a trained domain or imply that a math-domain selection validates general questions.

Use concise visible wording such as: “Research demo: fitted scores guide stopping; answers are not automatically checked.” Label q_current and p_next as fitted estimates, and model-reported confidence separately if displayed. A positive or negative estimated drift is a cost-sensitive myopic comparison; neither sign proves optimal stopping, future correctness, a safety guarantee, or improved answer quality.

Historical learned live runs all stopped at the two-response floor. The demo should allow the frozen rule to do the same and display that result honestly. A response-two STOP may be a near-fixed budget under this fitted artifact; it is not by itself evidence of problem-specific adaptation.
